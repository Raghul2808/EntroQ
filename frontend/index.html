1. Fundamental Physics: Born Rule vs Pseudo-Random Algorithms

Classical computers use Pseudo-Random Number Generators (PRNGs) such as the Mersenne Twister (random.random()) or linear congruential generators. PRNGs are entirely deterministic: given their internal state or seed, their entire future sequence is predictable and reversible.

In contrast, quantum randomness relies on the Born Rule. Applying a Hadamard gate transforms ground state |0⟩ into an exact equal superposition: |ψ⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩) / √2. When measured in the computational basis, the wavefunction collapses non-deterministically with probability P(|0⟩) = |⟨0|ψ⟩|² = 0.5 and P(|1⟩) = |⟨1|ψ⟩|² = 0.5. The outcome is fundamentally unpredictable according to quantum mechanics.

2. Elimination of Modulo Bias via Rejection Sampling

When an alphabet has size K and b = ⌈log₂ K⌉ quantum bits are measured, the measured integer v ∈ [0, 2ᵇ - 1]. Naive generators apply v % K. When 2ᵇ is not an exact multiple of K, lower indices receive higher probabilities (modulo bias).

This system enforces rejection sampling: any measurement v ≥ K is rejected, and an additional shot is gathered. Because every valid index 0 ≤ v < K has the exact same probability 1/2ᵇ, the conditional distribution is strictly uniform: P(v = i | v < K) = 1/K with zero bias.
